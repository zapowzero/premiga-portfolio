FAQ


The Paralympic Games were founded by Dr. Ludwig Guttmann, a German-born British neurologist. He organized an archery competition for World War II veterans with spinal cord injuries at Stoke Mandeville Hospital in England in 1948, which marked the beginning of the Paralympic movement.

The first official Paralympic Games took place in Rome, Italy, in 1960. They followed the Summer Olympics and featured 400 athletes from 23 countries competing in 8 sports.

As of late 2023, there have been 16 Summer Paralympic Games (beginning 1960) and 13 Winter Paralympic Games (beginning 1976).

The number of sports can vary slightly with each Games. Currently, there are 22 Summer Paralympic sports and 6 Winter Paralympic sports.

The Paralympic Games are held every four years, following the Olympic Games, in the same host city.

Many countries have hosted the Paralympic Games around the world, including Italy, Japan, Germany, Canada, South Korea, Spain, Australia, Greece, China, the UK, Brazil, and more.
